# Why Nettalabbo? 🤌🏻

The name Nettalabbo is a playful Italian take on the English words “Network Laboratory”.

It gently makes fun of the way Italians sometimes pronounce English words, including the developer himself, whose English back in the 1990s was certainly no exception.

You can almost hear the exaggerated Italian pronunciation, accompanied by that unmistakable gesture of pinched fingers, moving up and down, as if to say: “Ma che cosa? 🤌🏻”

There is a little affection, a little self irony and quite a lot of nostalgia in the name.

But above all, Nettalabbo is a fond and respectful tribute to NetLab95, the remarkable network utility created by Alexander Danileiko in the 1990s.

NetLab95 represented something wonderfully simple: a single place where you could explore, investigate and better understand the workings of the internet.

Nettalabbo was born out of admiration for that philosophy, rather than from an attempt to recreate the original application.

It takes that spirit in its own direction, introducing modern network tools alongside protocols from earlier chapters of internet history, including Gopher, Finger, Telnet and many others.

The idea is simple: a network laboratory should be somewhere you can experiment, learn, investigate and occasionally rediscover a little of the magic of the early internet.

Nettalabbo is an independent project. It is not a port of NetLab95, does not incorporate its original source code, and is not an official continuation or authorised version of the original software. No affiliation with its creator is claimed or implied.

It is simply a small, affectionate tribute to a program that deserved to be remembered.
