# Trademark precheck: Nettalabbo

Date: 19 November 2026. **Preliminary internet research, not a full clearance search.**

## Queries and findings

Search terms: `Nettalabbo`, `Netlabbo`, `NetLab95`, `NETLAB`, `NETLAB+`, and common spelling variants.
No immediately indexed exact third-party `Nettalabbo` result was found in the public web searches performed. **This does not establish that the term is free to use or register.** Search engines do not expose complete or current trademark registers, and there may be unregistered rights.

Network Development Group publicly identifies **NETLAB+** and **NETLAB Professional Edition** as registered trademarks in its own trademark usage guidance. Both concern software/network infrastructure training, although the products differ in purpose from Nettalabbo. This is a potential earlier-rights lead requiring jurisdiction-specific review, not proof of infringement. Some older US **NETLAB** applications are recorded as abandoned by secondary registers; an abandoned application does not negate other marks or unregistered rights.

## Clearance still necessary (not completed)

Perform authoritative, live name and similarity searches within UKIPO (United Kingdom), EUIPO/TMview (EU), WIPO (international marks), and, if distributed there, USPTO. Search exact and phonetic variants, including NETLABBO, NETTALABBO, NETLAB, NETLAB+, and variants that a consumer may confuse. Inspect registrations by **jurisdiction**, **status**, **owner**, **filing/priority date**, **goods and services** (notably Nice class 9 for downloadable software and class 42 for hosted software/services), and likelihood of confusion. Do not interpret a missing exact match as clearance.

No application has been filed or mark registered. Do not put ® or ™ next to Nettalabbo based on this precheck.

## Current practical recommendation

Use the distinct Nettalabbo name and independent Amiga-style branding while prototyping. Before broad public release or any trademark filing, get the live register results and consider professional review of the NETLAB+ similarity question. A historical-credit disclaimer cannot itself cure trademark confusion.
